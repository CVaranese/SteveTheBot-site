Re-screen at the new learning rate

11 Nov 2026 · T-12, T-22 · Inputs

Abstract

Doubling the learning rate and holding it constant had just made every model much stronger, so input variants compared at the old rate were tested again at the new one: a learned embedding of the animation inputs, and a rescaling of the largest inputs. The same batch asks whether doubling the rate once more helps. Eight self-play runs were trained, two per setting (plain inputs, the embedding, rescaled inputs, plain inputs at twice the rate), and each played a frozen copy of the strongest model so far. The plain runs scored far below expectation, winning 12% together; the frozen opponent has their settings and another seed, so it turned out to be an unusually strong run. The embedding runs won 50%, each above each plain run. Rescaling made no clear difference (21%), and twice the rate again was not clearly better and less steady (25%, with one run ending far less random than any other). In this batch the embedding leads, but two runs per setting differ widely, and the lead did not hold in the next experiment, which ran the same comparison at twice the length.

Question and hypothesis

The learning rate had just been doubled and held constant, which made every model much stronger. Input variants that had been compared at the old rate were therefore tested again at the new one: a learned embedding of the animation inputs, and a rescaling of the largest inputs. The same batch asks whether doubling the rate once more helps.

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Baseline (6e-4 constant, 4 seeds) vs frozen T21L6Cs4: mean about 45 (T21L6Cs4 is one of them, at the top).

Embedding: about +5 over the baseline (its old edge was small and the faster rate may cover the same ground).

Rescaling: about +5 (helps the first layer; small network, guess).

1.2e-3 constant: about +10 over 6e-4 constant; approx_kl about 0.008; risk of collapse (entropy below 2).

"Baseline" is plain inputs at the new default rate. "Frozen T21L6Cs4" is a saved copy of the strongest model so far, used as a fixed opponent; the numbers are wins out of 100 games against it. "approx_kl" measures how far one update moves the policy, and "entropy" how random the policy is.

Discussion

The plain runs of this batch scored far below the expectation against the frozen model. They won 12% together; the first quote expected a mean near even. The frozen opponent has the same settings as these runs and a different seed, so it was an unusually strong run of its setting.

The embedding scored well above plain inputs in this batch. The two embedding runs won 50% against the frozen model, and each of them is above each plain run ("Each run", Table 3). The second quote expected a small gain.

Rescaling the inputs made no clear difference. The rescaled runs won 21%, inside the spread of the two plain runs.

Twice the learning rate again was not clearly better, and less steady. The two runs at the higher rate won 25% with a wide gap between them ("Each run", Table 3), and Table 2 shows one of them ending with a much lower entropy than any other run.

Limitations

  • Two runs per setting. The gap between the two plain runs, and between the two runs at the higher rate, is large compared with the differences between settings.
  • An earlier run of the plain setting from the previous experiment scored higher against the frozen model than either plain run here. It is not part of this batch and is not in the tables.
  • The embedding's lead did not hold in the next experiment, which ran the same comparison at twice the length.
  • Only one model of this batch, an embedding run, was scored against the game's own computer player (Table 4).
  • The scores were measured on the copy saved at the end of each run.
