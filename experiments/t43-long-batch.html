Twelve times the usual training: 54 actions against the head controllers

15 Nov 2026 · T-43, T-42 · Controls

Abstract

Strength keeps rising about 100 Elo per doubling of training. Three heads are far behind; two heads catch up with the 54 actions.

The 54 actions gain about 100 Elo per doubling of training over five and a half doublings, with no ceiling in sight. Three heads climb half as fast and end about 330 Elo behind; two heads (one run) catch the 54 actions at about 200 million decisions. A usual-length run under-predicts the long one by 67 to 141 Elo. Against the level 9 CPU the gain shows only weakly: 11 to 19 wins of 50, against 21 for T4HumP1 at the usual length.

Question and hypothesis

Experiments in this project are normally judged after a fixed, short amount of training (the "usual length"). Two questions needed much longer runs. First, does strength keep rising in a regular way when a run trains 12 times as long, so that a short run could predict a long one? Second, does a richer controller, which gives the policy more actions to choose from, start lower but climb faster than the 54 fixed actions?

Written in the experiment plan on 14 Nov 2026, before the runs:

flat54 mean curve: still rising at the end, with less gain per doubling in the second half than in the first.

heads510 is below flat54 up to usual length (T-17: the 102 started behind). Whether it climbs faster is unknown: coin flip.

T43H102s1 lies between the two (one run; a difference under about 50 Elo from either says nothing).

Behind and not steeper: the 54 stay the default.

Speed and cost: 18,000 to 24,000 decisions/s per run, 6.7 to 9 hours.

In these quotes flat54 is the 54-action controller, heads510 the three-head controller and T43H102s1 the single run with two heads.

Discussion

Strength rose in a regular way, with no ceiling in sight. The mean curve of the three 54-action runs is close to a straight line against the logarithm of training: +100 Elo per doubling, with a straight-line fit of R2 0.98. The hypothesis expected less gain per doubling in the second half than in the first; the measured values are +87 in the first half and +93 in the second, so that part of the hypothesis was not confirmed. The fitted ceiling is far above the last measured point, which means the data do not show one.

A run of the usual length under-predicts the long run. A line through the first four checkpoints, extended to the end, lies below the measured end value for every controller. The extended line minus the measured value is -67 Elo for the 54 actions, -95 for three heads and -141 for two heads. The extended lines put the three controllers in the same order as the measured end values, but they do not show how close two heads come to the 54 actions.

Three heads started behind and did not climb faster. Their mean gained +49 Elo per doubling, and their difference to the 54 actions at the end is -331 Elo. Head to head against the 54-action runs, the end-of-run models won 21%. The plan's outcome for this case was "behind and not steeper".

Two heads reached the 54 actions late in training, on one run. The difference to the 54 actions at the end is -33 Elo, smaller than the bar the plan set for a single run to say anything. The result is "level late", not "better".

The three-head policies stayed closer to random. Their entropy at the end of training is higher, also as a share of the maximum for their action count (the training-runs table and the entropy figure). The entropy bonus had the same weight for all controllers, so it weighs more on a larger action set; this was noted in the plan and not corrected. Whether it caused the gap was not tested.

The gain shows only weakly against the game's own computer player. Against the level 9 computer player in the real game, the 54-action run won 11 of 50 games, the three-head run 11 of 50 games and the two-head run 5 of 50 games; the ladder run won 19 of 50 games. The ranking of the controllers there differs from the ranking on the ladder.

One unexplained result. The ladder run has the same settings as the three rated 54-action runs and another seed, yet its end-of-run copy sits at +481 Elo, well above their mean at the end (+332). Its Elo is fitted as an opponent, not as a rated run, and it is a single seed; no cause was established.

Limitations

  • Three runs for each of two controllers and one run for the third. The spread between runs of the same settings is large (the +- columns of the mean table), and the two-head result rests on a single run.
  • The Elo scale is internal: every ladder opponent is one of this project's own models, trained by self-play in the same simulator. Elo differences here are not differences against human players or other bots.
  • Only 12 of the 48 saved copies of each run were scored.
  • All runs trained in self-play only, and the entropy bonus was not adjusted for the size of the action set.
  • The straight-line shape is a description of these runs up to the length trained. It is not evidence about longer training.
  • One run per controller played the level 9 computer player.
  • The Elo numbers are from one fit, frozen on 15 Nov 2026. A fit with more games added later would move them by small amounts.

Decision

The 54 actions stay the default. The three-head controller is not dropped: its next test is a start from human replays (task T-42). Pending with the user: whether the two-head controller gets more runs, and the rule for reporting a setting's strength (the mean curve of at least 3 runs, no single "peak" number).
