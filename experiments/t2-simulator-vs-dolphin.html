Simulator against Dolphin

11 Nov 2026 · T-2, T-7 · Speed

Abstract

The project trains in a fast simulator of Melee and then plays the real game through the Dolphin emulator. That only works if the numbers the network is given, such as positions, damage, speeds and the current animation, are the same in both. This experiment recorded one emulator game with both characters driven through controllers, replayed it in the simulator from the recorded inputs, and compared the network's 30 input columns on every frame. The replay stayed together to the game's last frame, with stocks lost on the same frames on both sides. Before a fix, the differences were in hitstun, which the emulator reports outside real hit reactions, and in one animation frame counter. After the hitstun input was set to zero outside hit reactions, all but one of the 30 columns were equal on every frame, and the remaining differences were in that animation counter of one player. A game against the game's own computer player could not be replayed this way: the two sides drifted apart and the simulator's game ended long before the recording. The simulator's inputs therefore match the emulator's closely enough for models trained in the simulator to play in the real game.

Question and hypothesis

The project trains in a fast simulator of the game and then plays in the real game through the Dolphin emulator. That only works if the numbers the network is given (positions, damage, speeds, the current animation and so on) are the same in both. This experiment replays one recorded emulator game inside the simulator and compares those numbers on every frame.

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Run 3 (whole game): every field equal on at least 99 percent of frames, and the trajectories do not drift apart (the same stocks lost on the same frames).

Prediction: at most 2 of the 14 scalar fields show any mismatch.

Expectation for run 4 and its replay (written before): the two sides stay together for the whole game (the same stocks lost on the same frames, positions equal throughout); every field except hitstun equal on at least 99.9 percent of frames; hitstun differs only on frames where the player is not in a hit reaction.

The first two quotes were written before any run, for a game against the game's own computer player. The third was written during the experiment, after that first game could not be replayed, and before the two-controller game was recorded. "Hitstun" is the count of frames a character stays stunned after being hit. A "field" is one of the numbers of the state.

Setup

One game was recorded in the emulator with both characters driven through controllers, then replayed in the simulator from the recorded inputs. The state built on each side was compared per frame and per field. The comparison was run before and after a correction to the hitstun input. A second recorded game, against the game's own computer player, was replayed the same way.

Discussion

The two-controller game stays together to its last frame. Stocks are lost on the same frames on both sides (the two "stock changes" lines of the first group), and the simulator replay covers every frame of the recording.

Before the fix, the differences were in hitstun and in one animation counter. The hitstun lines show that the emulator reports a non-zero hitstun on many more frames than the simulator, and that no mismatch falls on a frame where the simulator's value is non-zero. This is what the third quote expected: the two sides disagree only outside real hit reactions. The fix sets the input to zero outside hit-reaction actions.

After the fix, the only differing cells are in the animation frame counter of one player. The after-fix line reports the remaining mismatching cells, all outside the first player's half. They are the same count the before-fix report gives for the second player's animation counter. The expectation of the third quote for that field was missed: the frames counted in that line are a larger share of the game than the quote allowed.

A game against the game's own computer player cannot be replayed this way. In that replay the two sides share only the first stock losses, and the simulator's game ends long before the recording does (the last group of lines).

Limitations

  • One game of each kind, one character, one stage.
  • The two-controller game was played by a random player and a scripted bot. Situations that such players rarely reach are covered thinly or not at all.
  • The comparison covers the inputs as the project builds them, not the full internal state of the game.
  • The cause of the animation counter difference was not established.
  • For the computer-player game, the comparison says only that this replay method fails; it does not test the state mapping after the two sides have drifted apart.

Decision

Training moved to the simulator; the hitstun fix is in the inputs.
