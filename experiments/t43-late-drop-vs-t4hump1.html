Do the long runs get worse against T4HumP1 late on?

15 Nov 2026 · T-43 · Yardsticks

Abstract

Late copies did win less (77% to 67%), but mostly in one run, and earlier copies swing as much; no clear decline with training.

Late copies won less against T4HumP1 (77% at 410 million decisions, 67% at the end), but most of the fall is one run (s2 fell 23 points, s1 fell 10, s3 rose 4) and earlier copies swing as much. Three runs cannot show a steady decline; the ladder Elo rose slightly over the same span. T4ImFull was dropped from the ladder the same day (every trained model beats it); no Elo value moved by more than 11.

Question and hypothesis

In the long batch, the runs with 54 actions kept rising on the project's ladder of fixed opponents. Against one of those opponents, T4HumP1, a model that started from human play, their later copies seemed to win less than copies from the middle of the run. If real, that would mean long self-play drifts away from what beats a human-style opponent even while it gets stronger against its own kind. This experiment plays new games to see whether the late dip is real.

Written in the experiment plan on 15 Nov 2026, before the runs:

c48 below c34 by 3 or more standard errors of the difference (pooled over 3 seeds, 600 games each, SE of the difference about 2.7 percentage points, so about 8 points): the late drop is real

Difference within about 2 SE: flat after c24, no drop -> the seed-1000 dip was noise.

c48 above c34: still improving slowly against T4HumP1.

My guess: flat (within 2 SE), c34 and c48 both about 65% to 70%.

"c24", "c34" and "c48" are the 24th, 34th and last saved copy of a run. "SE" is the standard error of a difference of two shares. "The seed-1000 dip" is the dip seen in the games already played with the usual seeds.

Setup

No training. Nine copies (three runs at three points of training) each played 200 new games against T4HumP1.

Discussion

The copies at the end won a smaller share than the copies at 410 million decisions. Pooled over the three runs the share is 77% at the middle point and 67% at the end. The first copy point, at 289 million decisions, is at 66%, about the level of the end.

The fall is uneven between the runs. "Each run" in Table 2 shows, for the three runs in order, the share at each point. One run falls a long way from the middle point to the end, one falls less, and one rises slightly. One run also rises from the first point to the middle point by more than the pooled fall that follows.

Three runs do not establish a decline with training. The pooled difference between the last two points is larger than the mark of the first quote, so for these copies it is not chance between games. But a single run moves between neighbouring points by as much as that difference, in both directions, so the result does not show a steady trend over training.

Limitations

  • Three runs and three points of training. The points between and after were not played with new games.
  • One opponent. The ladder Elo over the same span, which the abstract mentions, is from the long batch's analysis and is not shown on this page.
  • The intervals in Table 2 cover chance between games, not the movement of a run from one copy to the next.
  • The abstract's last sentence, on dropping a ladder opponent the same day, is a separate change to the analysis and is not shown here.

Decision

Nothing changes. Strength is still read as the mean Elo curve of several runs.
