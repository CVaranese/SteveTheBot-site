Three-head controller with a C-stick, built

14 Nov 2026 · T-42 · Controls

Abstract

The bot's 54 actions cannot express two things human players use all the time: the C-stick, which gives a smash attack on the ground or an aerial in the air while the main stick keeps steering, and partly tilted positions of the main stick. An earlier experiment added the partial tilts with a two-head output; this step adds a third head for the C-stick. It is a build with checks, not a comparison of strength. The network now picks a button out of 6, then a stick position out of 17 given the button, then a C-stick position out of 5 given both. The controller was built into the model code, the trainer, the imitation trainer and the players, checked with a script, and timed against the 54 actions in two short runs. The checks passed, and the simulator acts on the C-stick: smash attacks on the ground and aerials in the air. The three-head controller trains at the speed of the 54 actions, the two short runs within about one percent of each other. Five C-stick positions and no two buttons at once were choices of the build, open to change. Whether the controller makes a stronger bot was left to the long batch that followed.

Question and hypothesis

The bot's 54 actions cannot express two things human players use all the time: a second stick (the C-stick), which gives a smash attack on the ground or an aerial attack in the air while the main stick keeps steering, and partly tilted positions of the main stick. An earlier experiment added the partly tilted positions with a two-head output. This step adds a third head for the C-stick. It is a build with checks, not a comparison of strength: the question is whether the new controller works everywhere a model is trained or played, and what it costs in speed.

Written in the experiment plan on 14 Nov 2026, before the runs:

research/t42_heads/check_heads.py: 510 distinct triples; build, save, reload equal; ThreeHead equals P(b) x P(s | b) x P(c | b, s) by hand in NumPy; fresh model within 10% of uniform; history columns; simulator pad shows the delayed triple. Expected: all pass.

C-stick in the simulator: on the ground, C-stick up / down / side starts the up / down / forward smash; in the air, an aerial. Expected: yes (guess; if the simulator ignores it, the C-stick must be translated to A + stick on the frame, which loses drift control, and the user is told).

Speed, one run alone on the GPU, 128 matches: heads510 within 15% of flat54 decisions/s (guess).

"ThreeHead" is the new output layer. "P(b) x P(s | b) x P(c | b, s)" is the probability of a button, times that of a stick position given the button, times that of a C-stick position given both. "heads510" is the new controller and "flat54" the 54 actions.

Discussion

The three-head controller trains at the speed of the 54 actions. The two short runs are within about one percent of each other (Table 2), well inside the margin of the third quote. The record's table (Table 3) gives the decisions divided by the time in rollouts and updates.

The checks and the C-stick test are reported in the abstract, not shown here. The check script printed its results to the console and they were not saved to a file. The abstract states the outcome: the simulator acts on the C-stick, with smash attacks on the ground and aerials in the air.

Limitations

  • The outputs of the checks named in the first two quotes were not stored, so this page cannot show them.
  • The speed numbers were recomputed on 16 Nov 2026 from the stored training logs of the two short runs; the record's original figures, printed at the time by a slightly different rule, differed from these by about a tenth of a percent and were replaced.
  • Thirty updates of one run each: a speed check, not a measurement of learning.
  • Nothing here says whether the controller makes a stronger bot; that was tested in the long batch that followed. The game linked below is from that later run.
  • Five C-stick positions and no two buttons at once were choices of the build, open to change.

Decision

Design chosen by the user on 2026-11-13: two heads plus a C-stick head. Compared against the 54 actions in the long batch (the entry above). Still open: imitation from human replays with this controller, then PPO from that start.
