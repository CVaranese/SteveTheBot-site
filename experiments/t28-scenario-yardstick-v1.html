Scenario yardstick, version 1

12 Nov 2026 · T-28 · Yardsticks

Abstract

Whole games give one number per model, and runs of the same settings differ widely on it. This experiment builds a yardstick of single skills instead: every model is put into the same saved situations and scored on how often it gets the result a good player would. The first version covers three situations: finishing an opponent knocked off the stage (edgeguard), getting back to the stage (recovery), and following up a first hit. Start positions were saved from games, and random play and eight models were each put into the same 300 offstage and 300 opening starts, against a frozen model and against the scripted bot. Recovery does not separate the PPO models: all of them sit in a narrow band far above random play. Edgeguard scores are low for every model, only modestly above random play. For follow-ups the floor is high, since random play already lands one more hit in a large share of openings. Runs of the same setting are closer together here than on whole games. Watching the saved starts afterwards showed that the edgeguard and follow-up tests were too loose, because many starts were already decided, which led to a second version of the yardstick.

Question and hypothesis

Whole games give one number per model, and runs with the same settings differ widely on it. The idea here is to score single skills instead: put every model into the same saved situations and count how often it gets the result a good player would get. This first version covers three situations: finishing an opponent who has been knocked off the stage (edgeguard), getting back to the stage (recovery), and following up a first hit.

Written in the experiment plan on 12 Nov 2026, before the runs:

| random | 15% | 15% | 2 | 12% |

| plain 6e-4 constant (4 seeds) | 40% | 75% | 9 | 35% |

Order on every scenario: plain 6e-4 and embedding > PPO8Self > SimBase > random.

Follow-up ending in the stock: under 8% for every model.

Repeat noise (same model, other random numbers): 4 points or less on the share scores with about 300 starts.

Spread between the four same-setting seeds: about 10 points on the share scores.

The first two quotes are rows of the plan's table of guesses; their four numbers are, in order, the edgeguard score, the recovery score, the extra damage of a follow-up and the share of openings with one more hit, all against the frozen opponent. "Plain 6e-4 constant" is the four strongest plain-input models of that day, "PPO8Self" an earlier PPO model, "SimBase" a model of the 2017 learner.

Setup

Start positions were saved from games, then nine players (random play and eight models) were each put into the same 300 offstage starts and 300 opening starts, against two opponents: the frozen model T21L6Cs4 and the scripted bot at level 9.

Discussion

Recovery does not separate the PPO models. Against the frozen model every PPO model's recovery score is in a narrow band far above random play and the 2017-learner model (Table 3). The earlier model PPO8Self is level with the newer ones, against the order the third quote expected.

The edgeguard scores are low for every model. The best models are only modestly above random play (Table 3), and far below the guess in the second quote.

Follow-ups: the floor is high. Random play already gets one more hit in a large share of openings (Table 3), far more than the first quote guessed, so the trained models' lead on that measure is small. The share of follow-ups ending in the stock is above the mark of the fourth quote for the PPO models.

Runs of the same setting are closer together here than on whole games. The four plain models of the same setting (the two T21L6Cs and the two T22Base rows) differ by a few points on each share score against the frozen model, within the spread the last quote expected.

Limitations

  • The abstract's judgement that the tests were too loose comes from watching the saved starts afterwards and from the next version of the yardstick, which found that many offstage starts were already decided. That is not shown on this page.
  • The start positions come from this project's own models' games.
  • An opening start can be the first hit of a move that hits several times, which counts as "one more hit".
  • The frozen opponent is also one of the tested models and meets itself.
  • Models with a reaction delay or a decision every frame could not be scored by this version.

Decision

The user found the scenarios "pretty poor" after watching them and asked for version 2 (above).
