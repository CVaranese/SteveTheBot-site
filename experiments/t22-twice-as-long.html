Twice as long

12 Nov 2026 · T-22 · Method

Abstract

Question. Does training for 5,880 updates instead of 2,940 pay, and does it change which inputs win?

Finding. Twice as long pays strongly: each long run beats its own half-way copy 93 to 100 times in 100. The embedding falls behind plain inputs at both lengths (84 against 98 at double length).

Decision. Screening stays at 2,940 updates; double length is for a model to present. The embedding was dropped as default.

Question and hypothesis

Training runs so far had a fixed length of 2,940 updates. With the learning rate now held constant, runs were still improving when they stopped. This experiment trains twice as long and asks two things: how much stronger is a model at the end than the same run was half-way, and does the animation embedding, which led plain inputs in the previous batch, still lead at double length? It also scores the two best models of the usual length against the game's own computer player.

Written in the experiment plan on 12 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Longer pays: mean _c08 beats its own _c04 head to head about 65 to 35; vs frozen T21L6Cs4, _c08 mean about +15 over _c04 mean. Entropy at the end about 2.5, approx_kl about 0.005.

Embedding lead at _c04 vs frozen T21L6Cs4: about +20 over plain (the re-screen gave +30 with 2 seeds each). At _c08 the lead shrinks to about +10.

Level 9 in Dolphin: T21L6Cs4_c04 wins 45 to 50 of 50 (PPO8Bot_c04 won 48, PPO8Self 33); T22EmbSmall3_c04 about the same. Stocks lost per game below 2.

"_c04" is the copy of a run saved at 2,940 updates and "_c08" the copy at 5,880. "Frozen T21L6Cs4" is a saved copy of the best plain-input model of the usual length, used as a fixed opponent. "Level 9" in the third quote is the game's own computer player.

Setup

One batch of eight runs at double length: six with the embedding and two with plain inputs. Every run is scored at its half-way copy and at its end, so each gives a usual-length point and a double-length point from the same seed.

Discussion

Every run was far stronger at the end than half-way. Head to head against their own half-way copies the plain runs won 99% and the embedding runs 96% (Table 5; "Each run" shows no run far from the others). The first quote expected a much smaller margin. Against the frozen model the same step is from 54% to 98% for plain inputs and from 41% to 84% for the embedding.

The embedding did not keep its lead. Against the frozen model the embedding runs are below the plain runs at both lengths: 41% against 54% half-way, and 84% against 98% at the end. The second quote expected the embedding ahead at both.

The embedding runs spread widely half-way. "Each run" in Table 4 shows the six half-way embedding copies far apart, which is why two runs per setting in the previous batch could mislead.

Against the game's computer player the plain model of the usual length won nearly every game. It won 49 of 50 games; the embedding model of the usual length won 40 of 50 games.

Limitations

  • Two plain runs against six embedding runs.
  • The double-length models themselves were not scored against the game's computer player in this experiment; Table 3 holds the two usual-length models only.
  • A run's half-way copy is taken from a run with a constant learning rate, so it is a fair picture of a usual-length run of the same settings; with a learning rate that falls to zero this would not hold.
  • The frozen opponent is itself a plain-input model.
  • One doubling of the length. Longer runs were made in a later experiment.

Decision

Screening stays at 2,940 updates; double length is for a model to present. The embedding was dropped as default.
