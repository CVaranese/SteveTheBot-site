Scenario yardstick, version 2

12 Nov 2026 · T-31 · Yardsticks

Abstract

Question. Can saved positions test single skills (edgeguard, recovery, punishes) fairly?

Finding. Recovery is solved and edgeguarding is absent: the bots barely go after an opponent offstage. Starts already lost or already safe are filtered out by playing them on first (170 of 380 kept). The shielding test favours random play because the starts are moments these models were hit.

Decision. The user watched samples and accepted them ("they're just cut a little too long"); the scenario pages on this site play them.

Question and hypothesis

The first version of the scenario yardstick put every model into the same saved situations, but its edgeguard and follow-up scores were hard to trust: on watching the samples, many starts were already decided before the tested model could do anything. This second version judges edgeguard and recovery by what finally happens, removes starts that are already decided, and adds three tests of neutral play: punishing a missed attack, landing the first hit, and shielding an incoming move.

Written in the experiment plan on 12 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Replay equals the recording: 0 frames differ.

Offstage starts kept (recoverable unopposed): about 70% of the 380 evaluation starts.

Critic against rollouts: correlation about 0.5; its best threshold agrees with keep / drop about 75% of the time.

First hit is the test I expect to separate PPO8Self from the newer models (it loses whole games to them but equalled them on the three version 1 scenarios).

The two table rows give the plan's guesses for random play and for the strongest plain-input models. Their six numbers are, in order: edgeguard, recovery, share of openings with one more hit, whiff punish, first hit by the model minus first hit by the opponent, and shield "not hit". The "critic" is the strong model's value network, tried as a cheap way to tell which starts are still open; the "rollouts" are the replays that actually decide it.

Setup

The offstage and opening starts of version 1 were filtered, new starts for the three neutral tests were saved from recorded games, and random play and eight models were scored against the frozen model T21L6Cs4. The experiment was run twice on the same day: after the first run a watched sample showed that many offstage starts were already safe, the filter was made stricter, and edgeguard and recovery were scored again.

Discussion

Replaying a recorded game reproduces it exactly. No frame differs in any of the five pairings (Table 2), as the first quote expected.

Many saved offstage starts were already decided, in both directions. The first filter dropped the starts that were already lost (Table 3). The second found that a large part of the rest were already safe: the offstage player got back with no input, or in under a second (Table 4). Fewer than half of the starts are left. The plan had only expected starts that were already lost.

The critic is a weak stand-in for the rollouts. Its best threshold agrees with the rollouts on most starts, but saying "recoverable" every time would agree on nearly as many (Tables 3 and 4).

The models barely edgeguard. On the filtered starts every model's edgeguard score is close to that of random play (Table 5), below even the modest guess in the fifth quote.

Recovery separates trained models from untrained ones sharply. The PPO models get back in the great majority of starts; random play and the 2017-learner model almost never do (Table 5). With the first filter the gap was smaller, because those starts included ones that are safe with no input (Table 6).

The neutral tests have high floors, and the shield test is biased. Random play already punishes a missed attack in a fair share of starts, so the models' lead there is small. In the shield test the models score below random play. The shield starts are moments at which the model then playing was hit, and most of those players are these same models, so the test selects situations they handle badly.

First hit differs most between models. The share of starts in which the model lands the first hit ranges widely across the PPO models, including between models trained with the same settings (Table 5).

Limitations

  • All starts come from this project's own models' games; the shield starts in particular are selected against the tested models.
  • The edgeguard score now rests on fewer than half of the saved offstage starts.
  • Whether a start is "already decided" is judged with one strong model; another model might recover from starts it cannot, or the reverse.
  • The scores against the scripted bot were also made and are not shown on this page.
  • Models with a reaction delay or a decision every frame could not be scored by this version.

Decision

The user watched samples and accepted them ("they're just cut a little too long"); the scenario pages on this site play them.
