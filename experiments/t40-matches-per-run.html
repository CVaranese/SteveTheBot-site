Matches per run

13 Nov 2026 · T-40 · Speed

Abstract

All three end level against frozen T4V2; per hour, 256 matches at once is behind.

All three end level against frozen T4V2 (two seeds each, which differ by up to 32 of 200). Per game played, 64 matches learns fastest mid-run; per hour of a full machine, 64 and 128 are level and 256 is behind. Memory limits how many runs fit, so 6 runs of 128 matches play 13% to 20% more than 8 of 64. Speed-ups that keep the learning identical gave 9%.

Question and hypothesis

A training run plays many simulator matches at once and updates the network after each batch of play. How many runs fit on the machine is limited by memory, and each run has a large fixed memory cost, so a run with more matches gets more play out of the same memory. But with the amount of play held equal, more matches per update means fewer updates. This experiment asks whether a run with 128 or 256 matches at once learns as well as the usual 64, per game played and per hour of a full machine.

Written in the experiment plan on 13 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Expected (guess): at equal games, M128 within 5 wins of M64 and M256 5 to 15 below (fewer policy updates per game: each update's data comes from an older policy relative to the number of games). Per full-load hour, M128 ahead of M64 (each run is about 1.5 times as fast).

If M128 is not below M64 at equal games by more than the seed spread, it is proposed as the screening default (with a new plain baseline at 128).

"M64", "M128" and "M256" are runs with 64, 128 and 256 matches at once. "Equal games" means copies saved after the same number of decisions. A "full-load hour" is an hour with the machine filled with runs of that setting. "Wins" are out of 200 games against four frozen models.

Setup

One batch of six runs, two seeds per setting, all playing the same number of decisions. Each run saved eight copies at equal amounts of play, and four of them were scored. The speed of each setting was timed separately with the machine full of runs of that setting.

Discussion

At the end of the run the three settings are level. Against the four frozen models the runs with 64, 128 and 256 matches won 48%, 54% and 47%. The differences between the settings are of the size of the difference between the two seeds of one setting ("Each run", Table 3).

In the middle of the run, more matches at once learned more slowly per game. At the half-way copy the mean wins of 200 are 97.5 for 64 matches, 81 for 128 and 70.5 for 256 (Table 6, from the rows of Table 4). The first quote expected 128 matches close to 64 at equal games; half-way it is clearly behind.

Per hour of a full machine, 128 matches reaches the end sooner. A run of 128 matches finishes the same amount of play in 0.53 hours against 0.85 for 64 matches (Table 6), because six runs of 128 do more decisions per second in total than eight runs of 64 (Table 5).

The runs with 64 matches scored lower at the end than at their sixth copy. Both seeds show it in Table 4. With two seeds it is not established whether that is noise or a real peak.

Limitations

  • Two seeds per setting, and seeds differ by a wide margin at a single copy (Table 4).
  • The comparison per hour depends on how many runs of each setting are run at once; the page shows the speeds measured (Table 5) but not the memory readings behind the choice of 8, 6 and 4 runs.
  • Table 5 was recomputed on 16 Nov 2026 from the stored logs of the timing runs of 13 Nov 2026; the timing script's own printed summary was not saved.
  • The abstract's range for how much more six runs of 128 play than eight of 64 has two ends: the larger follows from Table 5, the smaller from an earlier timing whose output was not stored. Its last sentence, on speed-ups that leave learning unchanged, rests on timing output that was also not stored. Neither is shown here.
  • Table 2 covers each whole run; the copies scored in Table 4 are points along it.
  • One training length (the usual one). Whether the mid-run gap matters for longer runs was not tested.

Decision

128 matches is the default for new runs (the user, 2026-11-13): 6 runs per batch, about 0.6 h instead of 0.9, compared against plain runs at 128 from the same batch.
