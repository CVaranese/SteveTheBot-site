Faster experiments

11 Nov 2026 · T-19 · Speed

Abstract

Question. Can runs be shorter, and run many at once on the GPU, and still rank variants right?

Finding. Up to 8 runs share the GPU (71,000 decisions a second in all). Half-length runs are far weaker and rank variants differently, so they cannot screen. Doubling the learning rate at half length raised wins from about 4 to 31: the step size was holding learning back.

Decision. Screening stays at full length with two seeds per variant, 8 runs at once; the step-size result led to T-21.

Question and hypothesis

A batch of training runs took most of an hour on the processor. This experiment looks for ways to get an answer sooner: running several trainers at once on the graphics card, and making each run shorter. A shorter run is only useful for screening if it ranks the tested settings the way a full-length run does.

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Part A: one GPU run 18,000 to 22,000 decisions/s. Per-run speed falls slowly with more runs; the total keeps rising to at least 4 at once

Part B, full length vs frozen: baselines 45 to 60; EmbSmall 55 to 70; Emb2 below 45; Prev3 50 to 70.

Wins vs frozen 20 to 45 each: above the 1,470 checkpoints (12 to 29), since the learning rate is annealed, and below full length.

Rank correlation of the stock-difference order with the full-length order at least 0.7. If it is under 0.5, half-length screening is not supported and the recommendation is withdrawn.

lr 6e-4, 1,470 updates: 35 to 65 wins vs frozen, each within 15 of its full-length run; rank correlation with the full-length order at least 0.5; T19L6Prev3 in the top two, T19L6Emb2 in the last two.

If the lr 6e-4 runs stay under 20 wins, the reading is wrong and the number of games is the limit after all.

"Decisions/s" is decisions per second by one run. "Frozen" is a saved copy of the earlier model PPO8Self used as a fixed opponent, and the numbers after it are wins out of 100 games. "Full length" is 2,940 updates. "Emb2" is the run with an embedding started with a large spread, known from an earlier experiment to score badly; "Prev3" is the run that sees its previous action. The third and fourth quotes were written before the half-length runs, the fifth and sixth before the runs at higher learning rates.

Setup

Four parts. A timing test of runs sharing the machine. A batch of six full-length runs (four input settings). The same six settings again as runs planned for half the length. And the half-length runs once more at twice the learning rate, with two quarter-length runs at four times the rate, to test whether the total step size, not the amount of play, limits learning.

Discussion

The total speed rises up to eight runs sharing the graphics card. In Table 3 each of eight runs at once is slower than a run in the smaller groups, but far less than eight times slower, so the eight together do the most work per second. A single run was slower than the first quote expected.

Runs planned for half the length are far weaker than full-length runs. Against the frozen model the six half-length runs together won 4%, against 58% for the two full-length plain runs. The third quote expected the half-length runs well above what Table 4 shows.

Half-length runs do not keep the order of the settings. In the "Each run" column of Table 4 the half-length rows list their six runs in the order plain (two), embedding with a large start, embedding with a small start (two), previous action. The differences among them are a few games, and the setting that is clearly worst at full length is not last among them.

Twice the learning rate at half length recovered much of the loss. The six half-length runs at the doubled rate won 30% against the frozen model. The last quote set a mark below which the step-size reading would be wrong; five of the six runs are above it ("Each run", Table 4). The two quarter-length runs at four times the rate won 8%.

Limitations

  • One or two runs per setting at full length; the half-length rows pool six runs of four different settings.
  • The rank correlations named in the quotes were computed during the experiment and are not shown on this page; the "Each run" columns let a reader compare the orders.
  • The timing test ran while other jobs used the machine.
  • The opponents are this project's own models and scripted bot.
  • The frozen opponent was already beaten in about half the games by full-length plain runs; a stronger yardstick followed in the next experiment.

Decision

Screening stays at full length with two seeds per variant, 8 runs at once; the step-size result led to T-21.
