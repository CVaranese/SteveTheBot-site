Two-head controller

12 Nov 2026 · T-17 · Controls

Abstract

Question. Does choosing a button, then a stick position (102 combinations) beat the 54 fixed actions?

Finding. The 54 actions win: they beat both 102-action versions about 3 games in 4; two heads and one list are about even (48 of 100). All act every frame with an 11-frame delay and lose to the undelayed yardsticks.

Decision. The 54 actions stay the default; two heads is an option.

Question and hypothesis

The bot acts by choosing one of 54 fixed combinations of a button and a stick direction. That set has only full stick tilts, so the bot cannot walk slowly or use the attacks that need a half tilt. This experiment adds eight half tilts, which makes 102 combinations, and asks two things: do more stick positions help at the usual training length, and does it help to choose the button first and the stick second (two "heads") instead of choosing from one list of 102?

Written in the experiment plan on 12 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Delay-11 models are weak against undelayed opponents (T-16 delay 15: 0 of 100 vs frozen T21L6Cs4, 1 to 2 vs level 9 scripted). Guess: all six below 15 of 100 vs frozen, so the head-to-heads between the six are the main yardstick.

T17H102 vs T17F54 head-to-head: mean above 55 of 100 (finer stick control; guess, could be lower if 102 actions learn slower).

T17H102 vs T17F102: within noise (same-setting seeds differ by 12 to 13 wins), slight edge to two heads (guess).

"T17F54" is the 54-action setting, "T17H102" the two-head setting and "T17F102" the 102 actions in one list. "Frozen T21L6Cs4" is a saved copy of a strong earlier model with no reaction delay. All six runs have a reaction delay of 11 frames.

One batch of six runs, two per controller, with the same controls: a decision every frame and an 11-frame delay. The 102 actions in one list are the control that separates "more stick positions" from "two heads".

Discussion

The 54 actions beat both 102-action controllers head to head. Against the two models with 102 actions in one list the 54-action runs won 72%; against the two-head models they won 74%. Both 54-action runs are above even against both ("Each run", Tables 5 and 6). The second quote expected the two heads ahead.

Two heads and one list are about level with each other. The two-head models won 48% of their games against the one-list models. Each pairing was played one way only, with the first-named model as player 1, so the table has no row for the one-list models against the two-head models. The third quote expected a difference within noise.

Against opponents without delay every model of the batch lost almost every game. None won a game against the scripted bot at level 9 (Table 4), and Table 3 gives the few wins against the level 9 computer player. The first quote expected this.

Limitations

  • Two runs per controller, one training length. The larger action set may need more training than the smaller one; a later experiment ran these controllers much longer.
  • The head-to-head scores compare the six models of this batch only with each other.
  • One model per controller was scored against the computer player, with very few wins for any of them.
  • The experiment does not measure whether the trained models use the half tilts.
  • The games against the frozen model named in the first quote are not a column of this record and are not shown.
  • The scores were measured on the copy saved at the end of each run.
