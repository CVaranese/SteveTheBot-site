Long runs from human replays, and two more two-head runs

16 Nov 2026 · T-42, T-46 · Controls

Abstract

An earlier long batch trained three controllers from scratch for twelve times the usual length: three heads (button, stick and C-stick) stayed far behind the 54 fixed actions, and a single run of two heads caught up with them late. This experiment asks whether three heads do better when they start from a network that copied human replays, whether the two-head result holds over more runs, and whether the lead a human-replay start gave the 54 actions at the usual length lasts. An imitation model was trained for three heads, then eight runs of twelve times the usual length: three each of three heads and of the 54 actions from human replays, and two more of two heads from scratch. Strength was measured on a ladder of fixed opponents (Elo), against a pool of frozen models, head to head, and against Melee's level 9 computer player. The human-replay start kept the 54 actions ahead for the whole run, ending at the highest mean Elo of the five settings (+448, against +322 from scratch). It did not rescue three heads, which ended below three heads from scratch and won only 8% head to head against the 54 actions with the same start. Two heads ended level with the 54 actions over three runs (mean Elo +362). A human-replay start with the 54 actions is the strongest setting so far.

Question and hypothesis

An earlier long batch compared three controllers trained from scratch for twelve times the usual length. The three-head controller, which adds a C-stick and finer stick positions, stayed far behind the 54 fixed actions, and a single run of the two-head controller caught up with them late. Two questions were left. Would three heads do better when they start from a network that copied human replays, where the C-stick and partial tilts are in use from the first update? And was the one two-head run a lucky one? The same batch also shows whether the lead that a start from human replays gave the 54 actions at the usual length holds over long training.

Written in the experiment plan on 15 Nov 2026, before the runs:

T42HumH510 at or above T42HumF54 at the end, or catching up: imitation fixes three heads; they become a candidate default for the user to decide.

Behind by about the same as from scratch (about 330 Elo): three heads stay parked

Question 2. (a) T42H102 mean of 3 seeds within 50 Elo of T43F54 at the end, or above: two heads become a candidate default. (b) More than 100 Elo below: seed 1 was luck; two heads parked.

Human start vs scratch, 54 actions (T42HumF54 vs T43F54): ahead at usual length (T-4: 62 vs 48 against pool 1); whether the lead holds to 12 times is open, and is the T-46 / human-start answer at length.

"T42HumH510" are the three-head runs started from human replays, "T42HumF54" the 54-action runs started from human replays, "T42H102" the two-head runs and "T43F54" the 54-action runs from scratch. "Elo" is the strength scale of the project's ladder of fixed opponents. "Parked" means not developed further for now.

Discussion

A start from human replays keeps the 54 actions ahead for the whole run. The difference to the 54 actions from scratch is positive at every scored copy (the row "54 actions, imitation start" of the difference table), and at the end the runs from human replays hold the highest mean Elo of the five settings (+448 against +322 from scratch). Against the pool of frozen models they won 77% and the runs from scratch 63%. The last quote left open whether the lead would hold; it did.

A start from human replays does not rescue three heads. They start above three heads from scratch and end below them (-62 against -4), gaining less per doubling of training than any other setting (the shape table). Head to head against the 54 actions with the same start they win a small and falling share, 8% at the end. This is the outcome of the second quote, not the first.

Two heads are level with the 54 actions over three runs. Their mean Elo at the end is +362 against +322, inside the margin of the third quote's case (a), and head to head against the 54 actions from scratch they won 55% at the end. The spread between the three two-head runs is wide (the "+-" column of the mean table).

Against the game's computer player the 54 actions from human replays won the most games and three heads from human replays the fewest. Table 4 gives the games won at level 9 by the scored runs of each setting. The two scored two-head runs are far apart there ("Each run").

Limitations

  • Three runs per setting. The spread of the runs of one setting is of the size of some of the differences between settings (the "+-" columns of the mean table).
  • The runs from scratch were trained in an earlier batch than the runs they are compared with, with the same code and settings.
  • One of the three two-head runs is from that earlier batch.
  • Against the computer player one run per setting was played in this experiment; the table pools any other games of the listed runs against it.
  • The ladder's fixed opponents are this project's own models. The Elo scale says how the settings compare on that ladder, not how they would do against other players.
  • A further batch of this plan, with larger networks, was still running when this page was written; it is not part of this record.

Decision

The start from human replays is the default start for new runs (the owner, 2026-11-16). Pending: whether two heads become the default controller, and whether three heads are parked. A batch with larger networks (3 layers of 256) for the 54 actions and three heads is running.
