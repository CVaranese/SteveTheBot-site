Hand-edited weights

11 Nov 2026 · · Method

Abstract

Question. Can editing a trained network by hand (sharper choices, a wired-in recovery) make it play better?

Finding. Both edits lose fewer stocks to the level 9 CPU (2.05 to 1.62 and 1.34) and win more against frozen PPO8Self. Fully greedy play is worse: some randomness is needed, since the network cannot see its own last press.

Decision. The user watched it: it "definitely flails less". Hand edits stay a one-off; temperature tuning before scoring is queued.

Question and hypothesis

A trained network is a list of numbers, and those numbers can be changed directly. This experiment takes one trained model and edits its weights by hand, with no further training, in two ways that measurements of its play suggested: making its choices less random, and wiring in a fixed rule for returning to the stage. It asks whether either edit makes the model play better.

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

E2 (guess): sharpening with k between 1.5 and 2.5 lowers stocks lost per game against the level 3 scripted bot from about 1.16 to under 0.9 and shortens games; k of 4 or more is worse than the best k (stalls or timeouts).

E4 (guess): at least a third of lost stocks are failed recoveries or self-destructs rather than clean kills.

Expected (guess): level 9: the control near T-11's 3.96 taken / 2.10 lost, 48 of 50; the edits lose fewer stocks, 1.3 to 1.8 per game, and win at least 47 of 50.

In these quotes "k" is the factor the output layer is multiplied by, a "stock" is a life, "the control" is the unchanged model, and "T-11" is the earlier experiment that scored the unchanged model against the game's computer player. The first two quotes were written before any measurement; the third was added before the emulator games.

Setup

Discussion

Both edited models lose fewer stocks to the level 9 computer player. Table 2 gives the stocks lost per game: the unchanged model loses the most, the sharpened model fewer, and the sharpened model with the recovery circuit the fewest. The third quote expected the edited models inside a stated range of stocks lost; Table 2 shows both in it.

Games won against the computer player cannot separate the three. The unchanged model already won 94% of its games there; the edited models won 98% and 100%.

Against the frozen model both edits win clearly more games than the unchanged model. The unchanged model won 38%, the sharpened model 70% and the sharpened model with the circuit 64%. On this yardstick the circuit did not add to the sharpening.

Limitations

  • One model was edited. Whether the same edits help another model was tried only informally and is not shown here.
  • The size of the sharpening factor was chosen from a sweep against the scripted bot, and the abstract's remark that fully greedy play is worse comes from that sweep. The sweep is not shown on this page, and neither are the tests of the first two quotes.
  • The unchanged model's row pools two runs against the computer player (this experiment's control and the earlier experiment's run).
  • Stocks lost per game over this many games has a sampling error that the table does not show.
  • No training followed the edits.

Decision

The user watched it: it "definitely flails less". Hand edits stay a one-off; temperature tuning before scoring is queued.
