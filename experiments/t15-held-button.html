Does a held button stay held?

12 Nov 2026 · T-15 · Controls

Abstract

The bot chooses one of a fixed set of actions at every decision, and many of them include a button. If it chooses the same button at two decisions in a row, the game could see one long press or two separate presses, and the difference matters: a jab combo or a double jump needs the button released and pressed again. Before work on the bot's controls, a scripted schedule of inputs was played once in the real game, through the Dolphin emulator, and once in the simulator: the attack button and the jump button were each held over ten decisions, then alternated with no input. The same button at consecutive decisions was one continuous press: the attack started only the first jab and the jump only one jump. Alternating the button with no input gave separate presses, a full jab sequence and a second jump in the air. The emulator and the simulator traces agree line for line. With its action set, then, the bot cannot press a button twice in a row; it has to choose a different action in between, and the simulator reproduces this behaviour of the game exactly.

Question and hypothesis

The bot chooses one of a fixed set of actions at every decision, and many of those actions include a button. If it chooses the same button at two decisions in a row, the game might see one long press or two separate presses. The difference matters: a jab combo or a double jump needs the button released and pressed again. This check was made before work on the bot's controls, in the real game (through the Dolphin emulator) and in the simulator.

Written in the experiment plan on 12 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Dolphin, A held: pad_A is 1 on 20 consecutive frames, no 0 between decisions; Falcon starts jab 1 (ATTACK_11) once and no jab 2.

Dolphin, A pulsed: pad_A alternates 1,1,0,0; jab 1 then jab 2 (ATTACK_12) and more.

X held: one jump (KNEE_BEND once), no double jump (JUMP_AERIAL_*). X pulsed: jump then double jump.

Simulator: the same action sequence in every block.

"pad_A" is the A button as the game reports it (1 pressed, 0 released). A "jab" is the standing A attack, whose second hit needs a second press. "KNEE_BEND" is the start of a jump from the ground.

One scripted run in the emulator and one in the simulator, with the same schedule of inputs: a button held over ten decisions, then the same button alternated with no input, for the attack button and for the jump button.

Discussion

The same button at consecutive decisions is one continuous press. In the "held" blocks the button reads 1 on all 20 frames with no 0 between decisions, the attack block starts the first jab once and no second jab, and the jump block starts one jump and no second jump (Table 2). This is what the first and third quotes expected.

Alternating the button with no input gives separate presses. In the "pulsed" blocks the pattern alternates, the attack block goes on from the first jab to the second and third and the rapid jab, and the jump block starts a jump and then a second jump in the air.

The emulator and the simulator agree. The two halves of Table 2 are the same line for line.

Consequence for the bot. With its action set, the bot cannot press a button twice in a row; it must choose a different action in between.

Limitations

  • Two buttons, one character, one stage, standing on the ground at the start of each block.
  • One decision every 2 frames, the timing the bot used at that time. A decision every frame was not tested here.
  • The check shows what the game sees; it does not measure how much this limits a trained model.

Decision

Kept in mind for the controller work; no change made.
