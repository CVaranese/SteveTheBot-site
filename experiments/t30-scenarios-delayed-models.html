Scenarios for models with a reaction delay

13 Nov 2026 · T-30 · Yardsticks

Abstract

The scenario yardstick puts every model into the same saved positions and scores single skills. It was built when every model decided every second frame with no delay, and it refused the newer models, which decide every frame and react with an 11-frame delay. This experiment extended the yardstick to play every model with its own controls, checked that the extension changes nothing for the old models, and then scored 28 models against an undelayed frozen model. Inputs that need a previous frame were estimated from the saved speeds and animation counter, which was right far more often than starting them as at the beginning of a match. The delayed models did worse than the undelayed comparison on every scenario except edgeguarding, where every model scored low: they lost the first hit more often and rarely punished a missed attack. Recovery differed most between models, from about a fifth of the starts to more than four fifths, even between runs that differ only in their seed; the two runs held close to the human policy recovered best. The shield test cannot be passed by a delayed model, which stands still for its first 11 frames of each position, so its shield numbers are not comparable with those of undelayed models.

Question and hypothesis

The scenario yardstick puts every model into the same saved positions and scores single skills. It was built when all models decided every second frame with no delay, and it refused the newer models, which decide every frame and act with a reaction delay of 11 frames. This experiment extends the yardstick to play every model with its own controls, checks that the extension changes nothing for the old models, and then scores the delayed models.

Written in the experiment plan on 13 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Regression: a 2-frame model (T21L6Cs4_c04 vs frozen T21L6Cs4_c04, scenario v2) gives the same values as its rows already in logs/scenario.csv, exactly.

Tracker check, random play, 3,000 frames x 16 matches: option B movement equal to the true one (within 0.01) on at least 90% of player-frames; action count equal on at least 70%. Option A worse on both.

Delayed models (T4HumP1, T37Plain7): recovery 60% to 85% (2-frame models 83% to 92%), first hit 20% to 50%, whiff punish below the 2-frame models' 21% to 29%.

A "2-frame model" decides every second frame with no delay. "Option B" is estimating the inputs that need a previous frame from the saved speeds and animation counter; "option A" is starting them as at the beginning of a match. The models named in the third quote are examples of delayed models.

Discussion

The estimate from the saved speeds is far better than starting from nothing. In Table 2 the movement estimate is right on almost all frames and the action count on most, both above the marks of the second quote; starting as at a match start is right far less often.

Delayed models do worse than the undelayed comparison on every scenario but edgeguarding. Table 3 puts the frozen opponent itself, which has no delay, beside eight delayed models. Edgeguard scores are low for all of them alike. On recovery, whiff punish and first hit the delayed models are lower.

Recovery differs most between models, including models of the same setting. In Tables 4 and 5 the recovery column runs from about a fifth to more than four fifths. Runs that differ only in their seed are far apart (the T4V2 rows), and the two runs held close to the human policy (T4HumKa) have the highest recovery of the delayed models. The third quote expected a narrower and higher range.

The shield test cannot be passed by a delayed model. Its "shield: not hit" scores are at or near zero for every delayed model. The attack in that test lands in fewer frames than the model stands still for at the start of a position, so this number says nothing about the models' shielding.

Limitations

  • A delayed model starts every position with an empty delay queue and stands still for 11 frames. This costs it in every scenario, most in the short ones, and how much of the recovery gap it causes was not measured.
  • The first quote's check (unchanged scores for an old model) was made during the work; its comparison is not shown on this page.
  • The opponent has no delay, so the scores are not a comparison between delayed models and their own kind.
  • The positions come from games of earlier, undelayed models.
  • Two repeats per model; the spread between repeats is not shown in these files.
