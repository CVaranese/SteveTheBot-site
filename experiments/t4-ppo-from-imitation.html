PPO from imitation (the human line)

13 Nov 2026 · T-4 · Replays

Abstract

Question. Does starting PPO from a network that copied human Falcon replays beat starting from scratch?

Finding. Starting from imitation helps: mean 62 wins of 100 against frozen T4V2, against 48 from scratch, though the two seeds differ by 20, so the gain is likely, not firm. Imitation alone wins almost nothing (1 of 100). The frame-history start (47) and a pull back toward the imitation policy (38) were both worse. Against the level 9 CPU, T4HumP1 won 21 of 50; delayed models from scratch won 1 to 3.

Decision. PPO from imitation without a pull back is the best start found. Whether the imitation-based models look more human is for the user to watch.

Question and hypothesis

A network trained only to copy human replays cannot play: it loses every game. But it may still be a better place to start reinforcement learning from than random weights. This experiment starts PPO self-play from the imitation network and asks whether the result is stronger than PPO from scratch at the same length. It also tries two variations: starting from an imitation network that sees past frames, and holding the policy close to the human one during PPO.

Written in the experiment plan on 13 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Expected (guesses, written before): plain-start runs beat frozen T4V2s3-s6 with mean 55 to 75 wins of 100 (Zero-line plain T-37 runs scored mean 48.4 there); level 9 scripted 2 to 15 wins; history start vs plain start 45 to 60 (T-37 from scratch: history below plain; the imitation gain was small).

Low starting entropy may slow PPO: if the plain-start runs score below 48, that is a finding.

c = 0.1: entropy at the end 1.0 to 1.8; KL to T4ImFull at the end 0.2 to 0.6; mean vs T4V2s3-s6 50 to 70; vs T4HumP1/P2 40 to 55.

c = 1.0: entropy 0.7 to 1.2; KL 0.05 to 0.2; mean vs T4V2s3-s6 35 to 60 (held near the imitation model); vs T4HumP1/P2 30 to 50.

A penalised run beating the no-penalty runs head to head (above 55) would mean keeping the human habits helps strength, not only style.

"Plain start" is PPO from the plain imitation network. "Frozen T4V2s3-s6" are four saved models trained from scratch with the same controls, used as fixed opponents. "History start" is PPO from the imitation network with frame history. "c" is the weight of the pull back toward the imitation policy (T4ImFull), and "KL" measures how far the policy has moved from it. The first two quotes were written before the first batch, the others before the batch with the pull back.

Discussion

PPO from the imitation network scored above PPO from scratch, with a wide gap between its two runs. Against the four frozen models the runs from the plain imitation network won 62% and the runs from scratch 48%. "Each run" in Table 3 shows the two runs from imitation far apart, one of them close to the level of the runs from scratch, so two runs do not make the gain firm. The mean is inside the range of the first quote.

The imitation network alone wins almost nothing. Without PPO it won 1% against the frozen models, and the network with frame history 1%.

Starting from the network with frame history was worse. Those runs won 47% against the frozen models, and 34% head to head against the runs from the plain imitation network, below the range the first quote expected.

Holding the policy near the human one cost strength. With the weaker pull the runs won 38% against the frozen models; with the stronger pull, 2%, the level of the imitation network alone. Head to head against the runs without the pull they won 24% and 1%. No run with the pull reached the mark of the last quote. Figure 5 shows the entropy of the runs with the pull staying low.

Against the game's computer player the order is the same. The first run from imitation won 21 of 50 games, the first run with the weaker pull 12 of 50 games, and the imitation network alone 0 of 50 games.

Limitations

  • Two runs per setting, and the two runs from the plain imitation network differ widely. Two more seeds were trained in a later experiment.
  • The from-scratch reference comes from another batch of the same day, with other seeds.
  • One model per setting was scored against the computer player; the from-scratch reference was scored there separately and its row in Table 6 pools both of its runs.
  • The experiment measures strength. Whether a model that started from human play looks more human was left to watching.
  • How far each policy moved from the imitation network was printed during training and is not shown here.
  • The training logs of this experiment's runs hold some updates with a faulty clock reading (negative times), so the hours in Table 2 are slightly off.
  • The scores were measured on the copy saved at the end of each run.
