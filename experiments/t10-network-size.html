Network size, and the learning rate it needs

15 Nov 2026 · T-10 · Method

Abstract

Larger networks were not separated from today's 2x128 at the usual length; they need a lower learning rate.

At the learning rate tuned for the small network (6e-4), the larger networks took larger policy steps and the deep one fell far behind. At lower rates they came back: 3 layers of 256 at 1.5e-4 had the best mean on the pool and the most wins against the level 9 CPU, but by less than two seeds can separate (about 20 points). That run was still learning at the end (its randomness had fallen less than the small network's).

Question and hypothesis

The bot's policy and its critic are each a small fully connected network: two hidden layers of 128 units. The question is whether a wider network (two layers of 256) or a deeper one (three layers of 256) plays better after the same amount of training, with nothing else changed.

Written in the experiment plan on 15 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Noise: same-setting seeds differ by about 10 wins of 100, so 2-seed differences under about 20 are "not separated" (T-41).

Bigger networks: guess, 2x256 above 2x128 on pool 2; 3x256 about equal to 2x256. If a larger size is ahead by 20 or more (mean of 2 seeds): it becomes the candidate default (the user decides) and T-48 starts.

Expected (direction and decision): approx_kl at the end near 2x128's 0.005 at 3e-4 for 2x256 and at 1.5e-4 for 3x256 (a check of the guess that step size was the cause).

"Pool 2" is the fixed set of four saved opponents described under Setup. T-48 is a planned test of a recurrent network, which would only be worth starting if capacity turned out to matter. The third quote was written between the two batches, after the first batch had shown the larger networks taking larger policy steps.

Setup

Six variants, two training runs each, in two batches on the same day. The first batch trained the three sizes at the learning rate that had been tuned for the small network. The second batch repeated the two larger sizes at lower learning rates, with the same seeds; the small network was not trained again and is the baseline of both.

Discussion

The first batch was confounded with the learning rate. At the rate tuned for the small network, the larger networks changed their policy more per update (Figure 3; the last-100-updates column of Table 2), and the deep network scored far below the baseline on the pool (8% against 30%). That comparison measures the learning rate as much as the size.

At lower rates the larger networks recover. The deep network rose to 25% at half the rate and to 34% at a quarter of it, the highest pooled share of the six variants. Against the level 9 computer player the same variant won 11 of 50 games, the baseline 13 of 100 games.

No size is separated from the baseline. The plan's rule, written before the runs, calls a difference of two runs per variant "not separated" below about 20 points. No variant in Table 3 is ahead of the baseline by that much. The only variant further than that from the baseline is the deep network at the first learning rate, and it is behind. Two runs of the same settings can differ from each other by as much (the wider network at the first rate, "Each run" column). The hypothesis that the wider network would be ahead at the usual length was not confirmed.

The deep network at the lowest rate was still becoming more decided when training stopped. Its entropy at the end is the highest of the six variants (Table 2, Figure 4). Whether it would gain more than the baseline from longer training was not tested here.

Limitations

  • Two training runs per variant. The intervals in the tables cover chance between games only; the spread between runs of the same settings is larger and is the limit on what this experiment can separate.
  • One training length (the usual screening length). Network size may matter more at longer lengths; this experiment does not say.
  • The learning rate was varied only for the larger networks. The baseline was not trained at the lower rates.
  • Only the first run of each variant played the level 9 computer player, except the baseline (both runs).
  • The frozen pool is made of this project's own earlier models, so it measures strength against that family of play, not against human players.
  • The scores were measured on the copy saved at the end of each run, not on the best copy.

Decision

The user, 15 Nov 2026: two layers of 128 stay the default; size stays open and is tested again with the richer controller at 12 times the usual length (3 layers of 256 at 1.5e-4).
