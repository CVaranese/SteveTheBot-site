PPO against the in-game CPU

11 Nov 2026 · T-11 · Method

Abstract

The first PPO models had been trained and scored only in the simulator, against a hand-written scripted bot that they beat in nearly every game. This experiment asks how they do in the real game, in the Dolphin emulator, against Melee's own computer player, an opponent none of them trained against, and looks for a simulator yardstick they do not already max out. No model was trained. Four PPO models (two networks, each trained by self-play or against the scripted bot) and one model of the 2017 learner played the computer player at levels 3 and 9, and then a frozen copy of one PPO model in the simulator. Every PPO model won every game at level 3. At level 9 the 2017-learner model won 0 of 20 games, while the PPO models won from 25 of 50 games (the 2017 network, self-play) upward. The frozen model spread the PPO models out where the scripted bot could not: the other three won 48%, 27% and 38% against it. The two kinds of opponent ordered the models in opposite ways: the computer player favoured the models trained against the scripted bot, the frozen model the self-play ones. PPO is a large step up from the 2017 learner, and a frozen model replaced the scripted bot as the harder simulator yardstick.

Question and hypothesis

The first PPO models were trained and scored only inside the simulator, against a hand-written scripted bot that they beat in nearly every game. This experiment asks how they do in the real game against its own computer player, an opponent none of them trained against, and looks for a simulator yardstick that they do not already max out.

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Level 3: PPO models take clearly more stocks than T-1's best (1.27): guess 2.5 to 4 per game, PPO8Bot_c04 highest (100 of 100 against the scripted bot), winning some games.

Level 9: fewer stocks than at level 3; guess 0.5 to 2 per game, few or no wins.

Old SimBase_c08 at level 9: below 1 stock per game.

frozen PPO8Self_c04. Expected: PPO8Self_c04 against itself about 50 wins (sanity check, same network both sides; a large gap from 50 would mean a port/perspective bias); baselines and random 0 wins; other PPO models spread out between, which is what makes it a yardstick.

In these quotes a "stock" is a life (a game starts with four each), "T-1's best" is the best earlier model of the 2017 learner, "SimBase" is a 2017-learner model, and "frozen PPO8Self" is a saved copy of one PPO model used as a fixed opponent. The last quote is from the plan's section on the harder simulator yardstick.

Discussion

The PPO models win games against the level 9 computer player; the 2017 learner wins none. At level 9 the 2017-learner model won 0 of 20 games. The PPO models won 25 of 50 games and 45 of 50 games with the 2017 networks, and 33 of 50 games and 94 of 100 games with the fixed networks. The second quote expected few or no wins at level 9.

The frozen model spreads the PPO models out, as the plan intended. Against frozen PPO8Self the three other PPO models won 48%, 27% and 38%; the 2017-learner model won 0%. The table marks the model that is itself the frozen opponent as the opponent, so the sanity check of the last quote (that model against its own copy) is not shown on this page.

The two opponents order the models differently. Against the computer player the two models trained against the scripted bot have the higher scores (Table 2). Against the frozen self-play model the two self-play models have the higher scores (Table 3).

Limitations

  • One training run per setting. The order of two models on either yardstick is not separated from run-to-run variation.
  • The level 3 games are part of the experiment (the abstract reports them) but the site's score tables have no level 3 column for the in-game computer player, so they are not shown on this page.
  • In Table 2 the row of the fixed-network model trained against the scripted bot pools more games than this experiment played: it adds a second run of the same model against the computer player, made later the same day as the reference of another experiment. Games in the emulator are not repeatable, so the two runs are separate samples; the abstract does not quote that row.
  • The 2017-learner model played fewer games in the emulator than the PPO models (Table 2).
  • The frozen opponent is one of the four PPO models being compared.
  • One character and one stage.

Decision

The frozen-model yardstick became the routine score.
