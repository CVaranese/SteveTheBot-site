Larger learning rate

11 Nov 2026 · T-21 · Method

Abstract

Yes: from about 54 to about 92 wins of 100 against frozen PPO8Self.

Much better: from about 54 to about 92 of 100 against frozen PPO8Self. 0.0006 held constant is strongest and was still improving at the end.

Question and hypothesis

Earlier runs had shown very small policy changes per update, which suggested that the step size, not the amount of play, was limiting learning. This experiment asks two things at a fixed run length: does a learning rate twice as large give a stronger model, and does it matter whether the rate is lowered to zero over the run or held constant?

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Control: the five existing default runs (3e-4 linear, wins vs frozen 55, 54, 47, 52, 64; mean 54), two of them (T19Base3, T19Base4) on the same seeds and GPU.

6e-4 linear: mean 62 wins vs frozen over the four seeds (default pool 54), level 9 mean about 72. approx_kl about double the default (peak about 0.002).

6e-4 constant: about 58, below 6e-4 linear (no late fine-tuning); approx_kl stays near 0.002 to the end.

3e-4 constant: about 57, slightly above the default.

Rule for the verdict: 6e-4 becomes the default if its four runs average at least 60 vs frozen and none is below 47 (the T-12 rule).

In these quotes "frozen" is a saved copy of the earlier model PPO8Self, "level 9" is the project's hand-written scripted bot at its level 9 (not the game's own computer player), "linear" means the rate is lowered in a straight line to zero, and "the default" is a rate of 0.0003 lowered to zero. The "about 54" in the abstract is the mean of the five default runs named in the first quote; the tables below show the two of them that share seeds with the test runs.

Setup

Three test settings were trained in one batch. The control (the default rate, lowered to zero) was not trained again: two runs of an earlier batch of the same day, with the same seeds as the first two test runs, serve as the control. The trainer's code differed between the two batches only by the new schedule option.

Discussion

Every test setting scored above the control, and above its expected value. Against frozen PPO8Self the control won 58%; the doubled rate lowered to zero won 92%, the doubled rate held constant 96%, and the default rate held constant 90%. The expected values in the quotes above were all far lower than these.

The first yardstick was used up, so a stronger one was added. With the test settings winning nearly every game against frozen PPO8Self, that opponent could no longer tell them apart. Against the stronger frozen opponent (one of the test runs), the doubled rate held constant won 97%, the doubled rate lowered to zero 53%, the default rate held constant 48% and the control 26%.

Holding the rate constant was better, the opposite of the hypothesis. The plan expected the constant schedule to finish below the lowered one. On the stronger yardstick it finished well above it.

The results are consistent with the total step size deciding strength at this length. A rate lowered in a straight line to zero adds up, over the run, to half of the same rate held constant. So the doubled rate lowered to zero and the default rate held constant add up to the same total, and they scored close to each other on the stronger yardstick; the control has half that total and scored lowest; the doubled rate held constant has twice that total and scored highest. This experiment did not vary the total independently of the schedule.

The runs at the doubled constant rate had not finished learning. Their policy change per update did not fall over the run and their entropy was still falling when training stopped (the policy-change and entropy figures), unlike the runs whose rate was lowered to zero.

Limitations

  • Two runs for the control and for each constant-rate setting, four for the doubled rate lowered to zero. Runs of the same settings differ (the "Each run" columns).
  • The control runs come from an earlier batch, not the same one. Whether two GPU runs with the same seed start identically was not checked, so same-seed runs are treated as a paired sample, not a repeat.
  • The stronger frozen opponent is itself one of the four runs of the doubled rate lowered to zero; that run has no score against itself.
  • One run length. A setting that is ahead at this length is not shown to be ahead at longer lengths.
  • All opponents are this project's own models or its hand-written bot. The models of this experiment were not scored against the game's own computer player as part of it.
  • The scores were measured on the copy saved at the end of each run.

Decision

0.0006 held constant became the default ("We can make these the defaults").
