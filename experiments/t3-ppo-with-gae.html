PPO with GAE

11 Nov 2026 · T-3, T-8 · Method

Abstract

Yes: PPO wins 87 to 100 of 100 against the level 3 scripted bot after 20 minutes.

Yes: in 20 minutes PPO wins 87 to 100 of 100 against the level 3 scripted bot, which the 2017 learner never beat in 48,000 updates. PPO runs 15,400 decisions a second (the old learner 5,600).

Question and hypothesis

The project's original learner from 2017, a plain actor-critic method, stayed close to random play and never won a game against a simple scripted opponent. This experiment replaces the method with PPO (proximal policy optimisation) and GAE (generalised advantage estimation), played on many simulator matches at once, and asks whether the new method produces a model that wins. It also compares the 2017 networks with a version that has fixes to its activations, its critic output and its initial weights.

Written in the experiment plan on 11 Nov 2026, before the runs:

Expected 15,000 to 30,000 decisions per second (the brief's floor is 10,000; the baseline does 5,600 actions/s, 28 updates/s); GPU within 30% of CPU either way (guess: rollout is CPU-bound).

Expected (guess): entropy falls clearly below 3.9 (the baseline stayed at 3.93); the scripted-bot run takes at least 1.0 stock per game against the level 3 bot (baseline best 0.69) and wins some games; self-play at least 0.7.

In these quotes "the baseline" is the 2017 learner, "entropy" is the randomness of the policy (its maximum for 54 actions is about 3.99), "the scripted-bot run" is the run trained against the scripted bot, and "taken" counts stocks (lives) taken from the opponent per game. The first quote is about speed, the second about the training runs.

Setup

Four PPO runs: the 2017 networks and the fixed networks, each trained once against a copy of itself and once against the scripted bot. The two 2017-learner runs of the baseline experiment are the reference. The inputs, the actions and the reward are unchanged from the 2017 learner.

Discussion

PPO wins games that the 2017 learner never won. Against the scripted bot at level 3 the two 2017-learner runs won 0% and 0% of their games. The four PPO runs won 87%, 100%, 93% and 100%. The expectation asked for at least one stock per game and some wins; Table 3 shows both were exceeded.

The self-play runs win against an opponent they never trained against. The two self-play PPO runs never played the scripted bot in training and still won most games against it at level 3 (Table 3). The two runs trained against the scripted bot score highest, on their own training opponent.

This experiment does not separate the fixed networks from the 2017 networks. At level 3 the scores with and without the fixes are close for both kinds of training (Table 3), with one run each.

Entropy fell below the expected mark. Table 2 gives the entropy of each PPO run over its last 100 updates, which is below the value named in the second quote.

Limitations

  • One run per setting. Differences of a few games between PPO settings are not separated from run-to-run variation.
  • The yardstick is close to its ceiling for the PPO models at level 3, so it cannot rank them.
  • The models trained against the scripted bot are scored on their training opponent.
  • The speed numbers in the abstract come from separate three-minute timing runs on the processor, computed as all decisions over all elapsed time, and the old learner's speed from its own run. They are not shown on this page. The speed column of Table 2 is a different measure: the median update of the training runs.
  • The PPO runs stopped on wall-clock time, so their numbers of updates differ (Table 2).
  • No opponent here is the game's own computer player; that was scored in a separate experiment.

Decision

PPO with the network fixes became the learner.
